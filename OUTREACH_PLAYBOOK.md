# Outreach Playbook

Draft, generic staffing-industry-standard positioning and scripts for an independent/freelance
general-labor staffing operation. **Edit the bracketed placeholders and the differentiators
below to match reality** (your actual fee structure, turnaround time, candidate pool, etc.) —
these are solid industry-standard starting points, not claims already verified as true for you.

## Your differentiators (edit these first, then everything below will already make sense)

- **Speed**: candidates in front of the client within ~48 hours of a request.
- **Risk-free for the client**: no upfront fee — they only pay once someone is placed and working
  (a standard "contingency" staffing model). Consider a short guarantee window (e.g. replace free
  of charge if the placement doesn't work out in the first 5-10 working days).
- **Pre-screening**: every candidate is checked for availability, reliability (references/work
  history), and role fit before being sent over — the client isn't sorting through raw resumes.
- **Local market knowledge**: focused specifically on the Montreal/Quebec general-labor market
  (bilingual candidate pool, familiarity with local commute patterns/transit access, which matters
  a lot for warehouse/production shifts).
- **Personal, direct line**: as an independent operator (not a big agency), the client deals with
  you directly every time, not a rotating account rep — faster answers, more flexibility on
  short-notice or unusual requests.

## Cold-call do's and don'ts

**Don't:**
- Open with small talk ("How are you today?") — on a decision-maker who's busy, this reads as a
  scripted telemarketing call and increases hang-ups rather than warming things up.
- Say "I'm just calling to introduce myself/our services" — too vague; HR/ops people pattern-match
  this instantly as a generic cold call and tune out.
- Ask "Do you have a moment?" as your opener — it hands them an easy "no." Get to the point
  instead; if you need permission, ask for a specific small amount ("got 20 seconds?").
- Recite every differentiator in one breath. Pick 2-3 that match what they actually said.
- Talk over them or fill silence — a pause after your opener is doing work, let it sit.
- Badmouth their current agency or process, even if they complain about it themselves — agree
  with the pain, don't pile on.
- Leave a rambling voicemail. Under 20-25 seconds: who you are, why you're calling (one line),
  one callback reason, your number said clearly (twice if it's not obvious).
- End a call with nothing concrete. Even a "no" should end with a permission to email, a specific
  redial date, or a name to try instead — never just "ok, thanks, bye."

**Do:**
- Reference something specific to them in the first line (they're hiring for X role right now) —
  personalized beats generic every time.
- Get through your opener in under ~10-15 seconds, then stop talking and let them respond.
- Ask a discovery question early so they're talking, not just receiving a pitch.
- Match your energy to theirs — rushed manager gets the fast version, someone chatty gets more
  room to talk about their actual hiring pain.
- Log the outcome immediately after every call (see the tracking section) — don't rely on memory
  for "I'll call back later."

## Call openers — pick the one that fits what you know about them (a few variants, not just one)

**1. Observation-based** (you saw they're actively hiring — strongest when you have this):
> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I noticed [Company] has been hiring for [role]. I help
> companies fill general-labor roles quickly — pre-screened candidates, no fee unless someone's
> actually placed. Got 30 seconds?"

**2. Problem-first** (works even without knowing they're actively hiring right now):
> "Hi [Name], [Your Name] here. Quick question — are you finding it hard these days to fill
> warehouse/production shifts fast enough when someone doesn't show up or a rush order comes in?"
(then pause and actually listen — this opener lives or dies on the follow-up question, not the
pitch)

**3. Peer-comparison / social proof** (good once you have even one or two real clients to point to):
> "Hi [Name], I've been helping a few [industry/region] companies around [region] fill
> general-labor shifts quickly, and wanted to see if that'd be useful for [Company] too. Have you
> got a couple minutes?"

**4. Direct/no-frills** (fast, respects their time, works well with people who hate being sold to):
> "Hi [Name], I'll keep this short — I place general-labor workers for companies in the Montreal
> area, no fee unless someone's actually hired. Worth a quick conversation, or is now a bad time?"

**Value prop, once they engage (30-45 seconds, pick 2-3 that land — don't recite all of them):**
- "I can usually get qualified candidates in front of you within 48 hours."
- "You only pay once someone's placed and working — no upfront cost, no risk."
- "Every candidate is pre-screened before they get to you, so you're not sorting resumes."
- "I focus specifically on the Montreal/Quebec market, so I know the local labor pool."

**Discovery question — get them talking, listen for the actual pain:**
> "Out of curiosity, how are you handling recruitment for these roles right now — job boards,
> another agency, walk-ins?"

**Close — low-commitment ask, not a hard pitch:**
> "Would a quick 15-minute call this week make sense, to see if I can take some of that off your
> plate? No obligation — if it's not a fit, no worries."

**Objection handling:**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already work with an agency." | "That's great — a lot of my clients keep me as a backup for when their main agency can't deliver fast enough, or for last-minute shifts. Worth having a second option?" |
| "We handle it in-house." | "Makes sense for steady roles. I usually help most with sudden spikes or hard-to-fill shifts — mind if I check back when one of those comes up?" |
| "Not interested / too busy right now." | "Totally understand — mind if I send a quick email so you've got my info if that changes?" (this is a **win**, not a brush-off — get the email, send it same day) |
| "Send me something by email." | "Happy to — what's the best address?" Send it within the hour, while the call is still fresh in their mind. |
| "What's your fee?" | Be direct and specific once you have real numbers — vague pricing kills trust fast. |

## Getting past a gatekeeper to the actual decision-maker

Whoever answers the phone (receptionist, assistant, another employee) isn't the obstacle — treat
them as the person who can actually get you to the right desk.

- **Ask by function, not just by name**, since you often won't have the right name yet:
  > "Could you point me to whoever handles hiring decisions for warehouse/production staff —
  > or contract decisions with outside staffing providers?"
- **If asked "what's this about?", answer briefly and honestly** — don't be vague or evasive,
  that's what gets you screened out:
  > "I help companies fill general-labor roles quickly — wanted to see if that'd be useful for
  > [Company]."
- **If the decision-maker isn't available, ask to be forwarded or for their direct contact** —
  gatekeepers will often give an email far more readily than a phone transfer, since it's lower
  commitment for them:
  > "No problem — would you be able to forward my info along, or give me [Name]'s email so I can
  > follow up directly?"
- **If they take a message instead of giving contact info**, send a follow-up email addressed
  generically but explicitly easy to forward:
  > "Thanks for taking my call earlier — feel free to forward this along to whoever handles
  > [warehouse/production] staffing decisions if that's not you."
- **Be pleasant regardless of outcome.** The same gatekeeper often answers your next call too, and
  a good impression compounds.

## Cold email variants — pick based on the situation, or A/B them across similar leads

**A. Direct-to-decision-maker, value-forward** (use when you have a name and some signal they're
hiring):

> **Subject:** Quick question about [Company]'s hiring for [role]
> *(alt subjects: "Helping with [role] hiring at [Company]?" / "48-hour staffing for [role]")*
>
> Bonjour [Name],
>
> I noticed [Company] has been looking to fill [role] positions. I work with companies in the
> Montreal area to fill general-labor roles quickly — pre-screened candidates, and no fee unless
> someone's actually placed.
>
> Worth a quick call this week to see if I can help?
>
> [Your name] · [Phone] · [Email]

**B. "Please point me to the right person"** (use when sent to a generic info@ address or a
gatekeeper, when you don't yet know exactly who decides):

> **Subject:** Who handles staffing/hiring decisions at [Company]?
>
> Bonjour,
>
> I work with companies in the Montreal area to fill general-labor positions quickly (pre-screened
> candidates, no fee unless someone's placed), and wanted to reach out to whoever handles hiring
> or staffing-vendor decisions at [Company].
>
> Could you point me to the right person, or feel free to forward this along?
>
> [Your name] · [Phone] · [Email]

**C. Consultative / question-first** (lower pressure, invites a reply instead of a decision —
good as a first touch when you're not sure they're actively hiring):

> **Subject:** How are you handling [role] hiring these days?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick question — how's [Company] currently handling recruitment for [role] positions? Job
> boards, an agency, walk-ins?
>
> I ask because I help companies fill these roles fast (usually within 48 hours) with no fee
> unless someone's actually placed — happy to compare notes if useful, no pressure either way.
>
> [Your name]

**D. Peer-reference / social proof** (use once you have even one or two real clients to point to,
even anonymized):

> **Subject:** What's working for [Company]'s general-labor hiring?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been helping a few companies around Montreal fill warehouse/production roles quickly, and
> wanted to see if it's worth a conversation for [Company] too — pre-screened candidates, nothing
> owed unless someone's placed.
>
> Worth 10 minutes this week?
>
> [Your name] · [Phone] · [Email]

**Email writing rules that apply to all of the above:**
- Keep it under ~80 words. Nobody forwards or replies to a wall of text from a stranger.
- One clear ask per email (a call, a reply, a forward) — never stack multiple asks.
- Put the value prop in the first two lines; assume they won't read past that if it doesn't land.
- Skip the company backstory/bio entirely in a first email — that's a second-conversation topic.

## Follow-up sequence (once you've picked an opening variant)

**Touch 2 — Day 3-4** (no reply to the first email):
> **Subject:** Re: [original subject]
>
> Hi [Name], following up in case this got buried — still happy to help if you're short-staffed.
> Even a quick 10-minute call would tell us if it's worth pursuing.

**Touch 3 — Day 10-14** (final touch — this one often gets replies *because* it removes pressure):
> **Subject:** Should I close the loop?
>
> Hi [Name], I'll assume the timing isn't right and won't keep following up — but if hiring for
> [role] becomes a pain point down the line, feel free to reach out anytime.
>
> [Your name]

## Recommended cadence (call + email combined)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Cold call (pick an opener above). If no answer → matching email variant same day. |
| 3-4 | If no response yet → follow-up Touch 2, or a second call attempt. |
| 7 | If still nothing → second call attempt (different time of day than the first try). |
| 10-14 | Touch 3 (final, "closing the loop"). |
| — | No response after that → mark `Not Interested` / `Lost`, revisit in 60-90 days (a lot changes with a company's hiring needs in that window). |

This maps directly onto the outreach-tracking columns in the Sheet (`Status`, `Last Contact
Date`, `Last Contact Method`, `Next Follow-Up Date`, `Attempts`, `Follow-Up Notes`) — after each
touch, update those fields so the `Follow-Ups Due` tab always shows exactly who to contact today.
