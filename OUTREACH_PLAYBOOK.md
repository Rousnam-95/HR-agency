# Outreach Playbook

Draft, generic staffing-industry-standard positioning and scripts for an independent/freelance
general-labor staffing operation. **Edit the bracketed placeholders and the differentiators
below to match reality** (your actual fee structure, turnaround time, candidate pool, etc.) —
these are solid industry-standard starting points, not claims already verified as true for you.

## Your differentiators (edit these first, then the scripts below will already make sense)

- **Speed**: candidates in front of the client within ~48 hours of a request.
- **Risk-free for the client**: no upfront fee — they only pay once someone is placed and working
  (a standard "contingency" staffing model). Consider a short guarantee window (e.g. replace free
  of charge if the placement doesn't work out in the first 5-10 working days).
- **Pre-screening**: every candidate is checked for availability, reliability (references/work
  history), and role fit before being sent over — the client isn't sorting through raw resumes.
- **Local market knowledge**: focused specifically on the Montreal/Quebec general-labor market
  (bilingual candidate pool, familiarity with local commute patterns/transit access, which matters
  a lot for warehouse/production shifts).
- **Personal, direct line**: as an independent operator (not a big agency), the client deals with
  you directly every time, not a rotating account rep — faster answers, more flexibility on
  short-notice or unusual requests.

## Cold call script

**Opening — goal is just to not get hung up on:**
> "Bonjour, [Name] s'il vous plaît?" / "Hi, is this [Name] — the person who handles hiring
> decisions for warehouse/production staff at [Company]?"

If a gatekeeper answers: *"I'm calling about your general-labor hiring — could you point me to
whoever handles that?"*

**Intro — show you did your homework, don't sound like a random cold call:**
> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I noticed [Company] has been hiring for [role — e.g.
> manutentionnaires]. I help companies fill general-labor roles quickly with pre-screened
> candidates, and you don't pay anything unless someone's actually placed. Got 30 seconds?"

**Value prop (30-45 seconds, pick 2-3 that land, don't recite all of them):**
- "I can usually get qualified candidates in front of you within 48 hours."
- "You only pay once someone's placed and working — no upfront cost, no risk."
- "Every candidate is pre-screened before they get to you, so you're not sorting resumes."
- "I focus specifically on the Montreal/Quebec market, so I know the local labor pool."

**Discovery question — get them talking, listen for the actual pain:**
> "Out of curiosity, how are you handling recruitment for these roles right now — job boards,
> another agency, walk-ins?"

**Close — low-commitment ask, not a hard pitch:**
> "Would a quick 15-minute call this week make sense, to see if I can take some of that off your
> plate? No obligation — if it's not a fit, no worries."

**Objection handling:**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already work with an agency." | "That's great — a lot of my clients keep me as a backup for when their main agency can't deliver fast enough, or for last-minute shifts. Worth having a second option?" |
| "We handle it in-house." | "Makes sense for steady roles. I usually help most with sudden spikes or hard-to-fill shifts — mind if I check back when one of those comes up?" |
| "Not interested / too busy right now." | "Totally understand — mind if I send a quick email so you've got my info if that changes?" (this is a **win**, not a brush-off — get the email, send it same day) |
| "Send me something by email." | "Happy to — what's the best address?" Send it within the hour, while the call is still fresh in their mind. |
| "What's your fee?" | Be direct and specific once you have real numbers — vague pricing kills trust fast. |

Log the outcome immediately after every call (see tracking section below) — don't rely on memory
for "I'll call back later."

## Cold email sequence (3-touch)

**Email 1 — Day 0** (either right after a no-answer call, or as the first touch on its own):

> **Subject:** Quick question about [Company]'s hiring for [role]
>
> Bonjour [Name],
>
> I noticed [Company] has been looking to fill [role] positions. I work with companies in the
> Montreal area to fill general-labor roles quickly — pre-screened candidates, and no fee unless
> someone's actually placed.
>
> Worth a quick call this week to see if I can help?
>
> [Your name] · [Phone] · [Email]

**Email 2 — Day 3-4** (no reply to Email 1):

> **Subject:** Re: hiring for [role] at [Company]
>
> Hi [Name], following up in case this got buried — still happy to help if you're short-staffed.
> Even a quick 10-minute call would tell us if it's worth pursuing.

**Email 3 — Day 10-14** (final touch, no reply yet — this one often gets replies *because* it
removes pressure):

> **Subject:** Should I close the loop?
>
> Hi [Name], I'll assume the timing isn't right and won't keep following up — but if hiring for
> [role] becomes a pain point down the line, feel free to reach out anytime.
>
> [Your name]

## Recommended cadence (call + email combined)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Cold call. If no answer → Email 1 same day. |
| 3-4 | If no response yet → Email 2, or a second call attempt. |
| 7 | If still nothing → second call attempt (different time of day than the first try). |
| 10-14 | Email 3 (final, "closing the loop"). |
| — | No response after that → mark `Not Interested` / `Lost`, revisit in 60-90 days (a lot changes with a company's hiring needs in that window). |

This maps directly onto the outreach-tracking columns being added to the Sheet (`Status`,
`Last Contact Date`, `Last Contact Method`, `Next Follow-Up Date`, `Attempts`, `Follow-Up Notes`)
— after each touch, update those fields so the `Follow-Ups Due` tab always shows exactly who to
contact today.
